What the book is about

When the harbor authority of Halvern Bay hires Ines Varga to produce a definitive survey, she expects a quiet season of tide tables and triangulation. Instead, the shoreline she measured in spring is gone by autumn, and a hand she doesn’t recognize has been correcting her maps.

The Salt Cartographer follows Ines through one year on the bay: the fishing families who distrust her, the retired surveyor who knows more than he says, and the slow realization that a map is a promise someone has to keep.

About the author

Mara Ellison spent eleven years as a coastal surveyor before she started writing fiction. She grew up on the northern shore, where her grandfather repaired nets and kept a drawer of hand-drawn charts she still uses for reference.

Her first novel, Low Water, won the Harborlight Prize for debut fiction. She now lives in a lighthouse keeper’s cottage with a very opinionated cat.
